Self-Reflective Planning: Enhancing LLM Reasoning Reliability
for Knowledge Graph Question Answering
Anonymous Author(s)
ABSTRACT
Knowledge graph question answering (KGQA) requires retriev-
ing relevant facts from structured knowledge to support reliable
reasoning. While large language models (LLMs) augmented with
knowledge graphs (KGs) have shown promise, existing methods
suffer from two critical limitations: (1) generating reasoning paths
that fail to align with the actual KG structure, and (2) the inability
to adaptively correct erroneous paths based on retrieval feedback.
These limitations lead to unreliable knowledge retrieval and de-
graded answer quality. We propose Self-Reflective Planning (SRP),
a novel framework that enhances reasoning reliability through
reference-guided planning and iterative self-reflection. SRP consists
of four tightly coupled modules. A Reference Searching module
retrieves task-relevant reference instances that capture success-
ful question-solving patterns, providing high-level guidance for
reasoning path generation. Guided by these references, a Path Plan-
ning module generates structure-aligned reasoning paths, reducing
structural hallucinations. A Knowledge Retrieval module then exe-
cutes the planned reasoning paths to retrieve candidate knowledge
from the KG, enabling path-level verification through concrete ev-
idence. Finally, a Reflection and Reasoning module evaluates the
retrieved knowledge against the question and iteratively revises
erroneous reasoning paths based on retrieval feedback, enabling
self-corrective reasoning. Extensive experiments on WebQSP, CWQ,
and GrailQA demonstrate that SRP achieves state-of-the-art results,
outperforming the strongest baseline by up to 14.6% on CWQ.

INTRODUCTION
Recent breakthroughs in large language models (LLMs) have signif-
icantly advanced natural language processing [36, 38, 39]. Nonethe-
less, LLMs are prone to hallucination, a phenomenon where the
model generates incorrect or fabricated information. This issue
often arises when the knowledge required to address a problem ex-
ceeds what the model has acquired during pretraining. [13] When
the knowledge required for a specific question falls outside its
pretraining scope, LLMs may invent a false answer rather than
acknowledge its uncertainty. Research into hallucinations is a key
focus in current LLM studies, as it enhances LLMs’ capacity for
reliable reasoning.
To tackle this critical problem, researchers attempt to integrate
LLMs with knowledge graphs (KGs), capitalizing on their explicit
structure and rich semantic knowledge to reinforce factual ground-
ing. KGs provide a structured representation of real-world facts in
the form of triples, which offers advantages such as interpretability
and traceability that are often lacking in the implicit, parametric
knowledge of LLMs. Prior research has explored two primary cate-
gories of approaches: Fine-tuning methods and Prompting-based
methods. Fine-tuning methods equip models with the ability to uti-
lize information in KGs by encoding structured knowledge directly
into model parameters, often leveraging techniques like knowl-
edge graph embeddings or graph neural networks [7, 32]. While
effective for internalizing knowledge, these methods are compu-
tationally expensive and struggle to incorporate new knowledge
without retraining. Prompt-based methods involve guiding LLMs
with strategic prompts to make plans and decisions, enabling them
to search KGs for external knowledge in order to answer questions
[6, 19]. While these approaches are highly flexible and can access
substantial and associated knowledge from KGs, their effectiveness
depends heavily on the quality of the prompts and the underlying
knowledge retrieval system.
Despite their effectiveness, existing approaches still face notable
challenges. On the one hand, fine-tuning-based models require
high-quality, large-scale data for fine-tuning. Such requirements
not only limit their adaptability to new domains but also demand
substantial computational resources [11]. On the other hand, al-
though prompt-based methods can generate reasoning processes
to retrieve knowledge from KGs, they often struggle to identify and
correct shortcomings in the reasoning process when the process
does not fully match the question. Consequently, the knowledge
retrieved from the KG may be imprecise. For example, as illustrated
in Figure 1, given the question “Where was the author of ‘Profiles in
Courage’ raised?”, the model generates a reasoning path to retrieve
relevant knowledge from the KG. Following this path, the model
obtains the answer “Washington, D.C.”, which is incorrect. This
error arises because the relation person.places_lived refers to
locations where a person lived, whereas the question asks where
the person was raised. As a result, the model produces an erroneous

To address these challenges, we propose Self-Reflective Plan-
ning (SRP), a KG-enhanced LLM framework with reliable planning
and reflection ability. Specifically, SRP first leverages a Reference
Searching module to extract relevant instances from a reference
base, which serve to guide the model’s subsequent planning and re-
flection processes. Subsequently, through a Path Planning module,
the SRP identifies relevant relations to the problem as the initial
relations from those directly connected to the topic entities, and
then generates reasoning paths based on the initial relations as
the basis for retrieval. Thereafter, guided by reasoning paths, SRP
invokes a Knowledge Retrieval module to fetch the actual relations
and entities present in KGs. More importantly, to achieve reliable
reflection, SRP employs a Reflection and Reasoning module to iter-
atively judge retrieved knowledge from KGs and edit the reasoning
path based on judgement result. If the knowledge retrieved con-
tains the answer to the question or reaches the upper limitation of
predefined reflection times, then SRP stops reflecting and provides
an answer to the question.
In summary, the main contributions of our work can be summa-
rized as follows.
• We explore methods for reliable planning and reflection based
on referable instances to improve LLM reasoning over KGs,
thereby providing a new perspective for developing more ro-
bust question-answering algorithms.
• We propose a Self-Reflective Planning (SRP) framework that
can generate effective reasoning paths and refine reasoning
errors through reliable reflection.
• Extensive evaluations on WebQSP, CWQ, and GrailQA datasets
demonstrate SRP’s superior performance compared to competi-
tive baselines, underscoring the reliability and accuracy of SRP
in complex reasoning task over KGs. The code is available on
https://anonymous.4open.science/r/SRP-E06C.

RELATED WORK
2.1
LLM Reasoning
LLMs have demonstrated powerful performance in various domains,
greatly simplifying many traditional NLP tasks [10]. Benefiting
from a large number of parameters and extensive corpora accu-
mulated during pre-training, LLMs possess impressive reasoning
capabilities, enabling them to accurately solve complex problems
in various domains. To unlock and enhance these abilities, numer-
ous researchers have directed LLMs to incorporate their thought
processes into their outputs rather than merely delivering direct
answers [12, 17, 37]. A pioneering work in this area is the Chain
of Thought (CoT) framework [37], which encourages LLMs to gen-
erate a series of intermediate reasoning steps in natural language
by providing a few examples. This approach significantly improves
performance on complex reasoning tasks, even when LLMs are
not explicitly fine-tuned for them. Subsequent adaptations of CoT
reasoning, such as Self-Consistency [34], Tree-of-Thought [41],
and Graph-of-Thought [3], were introduced to further motivate
and refine the reasoning abilities of LLMs by exploring multiple
reasoning paths or aggregating diverse thought processes. These
methods underscore a critical shift from simply generating answers
to explicitly modeling the reasoning process, which has become a
key area of research.
Building on these advances, a new frontier in LLM reasoning in-
volves integrating external knowledge and implementing cognitive-
like self-correction mechanisms. One effective approach to enhance
reasoning is to leverage external KGs, where LLMs navigate the
paths of interconnected nodes and edges to reveal causal relation-
ships and extract contextual information that is not readily avail-
able in their internal parameters [16]. This integration enables
LLMs to base their reasoning on structured, verifiable knowledge,
thereby enhancing accuracy and reducing factual errors. Further-
more, to facilitate a cognitive process that more closely parallels
human thinking, many studies [5, 9, 22, 27, 35] have devised self-
correction mechanisms. These frameworks use feedback to amend
faulty reasoning and ensure precision by enabling LLMs to reflect
on their own outputs and iteratively refine their answers. This self-
corrective feedback loop is crucial for handling complex, multi-step
problems where initial attempts may fail. Our work is inspired by
this paradigm and proposes a novel self-reflection mechanism that
empowers the model to better plan and correct its reasoning paths,
specifically for the task of searching for effective knowledge within
complex KGs.

2.2
KG-enhanced LLM
While LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in many
areas, they are inherently limited by their static training data, lead-
ing to issues such as outdated information, hallucinations, and
a lack of interpretability in their decision-making processes [44–
46]. A promising paradigm to mitigate these shortcomings is to
integrate LLMs with KGs, which provide explicit, verifiable, and
dynamic knowledge. Early research in this area focused on end-to-
end approaches, where models were trained to internalize factual
knowledge from KGs directly through pre-training or fine-tuning
[21, 33]. These methods effectively enhanced the models’ reasoning
ability over KGs to comprehend language and perform complex
reasoning by grounding their knowledge in a structured format.
However, they were computationally expensive and struggled to
adapt when faced with novel entities or relations not present in the
original training or fine-tuning datasets, hindering their real-world
applicability and scalability.
To overcome the static nature and resource demands of pre-
training methods, recent research has shifted towards lightweight,
retrieval-augmented approaches that leverage in-context learning.
This involves designing specific prompt templates that guide LLMs
to perform complex reasoning over KGs without the need for exten-
sive training [1, 40]. For example, the ToG framework [28] enables
an interactive beam search on KGs, allowing the model to iteratively
explore entities and relations to find the optimal reasoning path and
return the inference results. Similarly, Readi [6] utilizes LLMs first
to generate hypothetical reasoning paths and then attempts to in-
stantiate them within the KG. While innovative, this approach often
faces a critical challenge: a high failure rate in accurately extracting
the question-relevant knowledge from the KG, which significantly
decreases the reliability of the generated paths. To address this spe-
cific limitation, our work introduces a novel method that enhances
the reliability of reasoning paths by employing a multi-step veri-
fication process, including reference searching, checking the first
relation, and self-reflection. This mechanism proves to be highly
effective for retrieving and utilizing relevant knowledge from KGs,
thereby improving the overall accuracy and trustworthiness of the
reasoning process.

PROBLEM DEFINITION
Combining KGs with LLMs to answer questions belongs to the
knowledge graph question answering (KGQA) task, which is about
resolving natural language questions with KGs. A KG is a set of
triples, i.e., {(𝑒,𝑟,𝑒′)|𝑒,𝑒′ ∈E,𝑟∈R}, where E and R are the sets
of entities and relations. Given a question 𝑞, a set of topic entities
T𝑞extracted within 𝑞, and a KG G, the objective of KGQA is to
infer valid answers A𝑞. To achieve this, LLM needs to plan reliable
reasoning paths, retrieve the triplet sequences based on reasoning
paths, and answer questions with triplet sequences. The reasoning
path and triplet sequence are defined as below.
• Reasoning Path. A reasoning path is a representation of a
reasoning process generated by LLMs, employed to retrieve
relevant knowledge within KGs. It is a sequence starting from
entity and followed by one or more relations, e.g., 𝑝= 𝑒0 →
ˆ𝑟0 →ˆ𝑟1 →· · · →ˆ𝑟𝑙, where each ˆ𝑟𝑖is a predicted relation
generated by LLM and 𝑙is the length of reasoning path.
• Triplet Sequence. Triplet Sequence is a sequence of triplets
retrieved from KGs. Given reasoning path 𝑝, the triplet sequence
is 𝑆= {(𝑒𝑑, 𝑟𝑑, 𝑒𝑑+1)}𝐷

METHODOLOGY
4.1
Overview
As shown in Figure 2, our SRP includes four modules: 1) Reference
Searching module that searches for reference relevant to questions,
2) Path Planning module that generates reasoning paths, 3) Knowl-
edge Retrieval module that retrieves triplet sequences from KGs
and 4) Reflection and Reasoning module that performs judgement,
editing and answering.
Given a question, module 1) first searches for references to guide
module 2) and 4). Then, module 2) generates initial reasoning path.
Module 3) bridges module 2) and module 4), achieving iterative
retrieval and refinement of reasoning paths.
4.2
Reference Searching
To guide the reasoning process, the Reference Searching module
retrieves relevant instances from a reference base, which contains
numerous referable cases about the question-solving process with
KGs. An instance includes a question, reasoning paths that can
be utilized to retrieve answers in KGs and the correct answers to
this question, which is denoted as reference. We extract references
from training set. To construct the reference base, each question
in training set is encoded into dense vectors with a pretrained
language model and then clustered (e.g., via K-Means). We randomly
select the same number of references from each cluster and store
them in the reference base.
During the process of planning and self-reflection, the model
encodes the input question and compares it against the embeddings
of questions from reference base using similarity-based search (e.g.,
K-Nearest Neighbors) to retrieve the Top-k most similar correspond-
ing references. These references are integrated into downstream
modules to enrich the reasoning process with relevant historical
contexts, as illustrated in Figure 2, thereby motivating the reasoning
ability of LLM to enhance the accuracy and reliability of planning
and self-reflection.

5.4
Reliability Analysis
To further substantiate the reliability of our proposed SRP frame-
work, we evaluate both the searching success rate and the reliable
answering rate of SRP and the second-best prompting LLM method,
Readi, across three datasets. The searching success rate is defined
as the proportion of times a method successfully retrieves a triplet
containing the answer to a query from the KG, divided by the
total number of question answering. The reliable answering rate
is defined as the proportion of correct answers supported by fac-
tual triplets in the KG among all correct answers. Together, these
two metrics reflect the overall reliability of the reasoning process
generated by each method over GPT3.5 and GPT4.1-mini.
Figure 3a presents the searching success rate for Readi-GPT3.5,
Readi-GPT4.1-mini, SRP-GPT3.5, and SRP-GPT4.1-mini. The data
illustrate that SRP’s performance of retrieving valuable knowledge
from KGs substantially exceeds Readi on three datasets. Even when
Readi uses GPT4.1-mini, Readi’s searching success rate is still lower
than SRP-GPT3.5 on WebQSP, CWQ, and GrailQA, which demon-
strates that the reasoning paths generated by SRP through reliable
planning and reflection are more effective in retrieving the knowl-
edge needed to answer the question.

question of whether structured KG-enhanced frameworks remain
necessary for multi-hop reasoning.To explore this issue, we compare
SRP with representative LLM-only baselines, including a reasoning-
enabled model and a larger model with better pre-training.

6.2
On the Fairness of Comparisons with PoG-2
We observe that some recent advanced KG-enhanced reasoning
models, such as PoG-21 [30], achieve stronger performance than
SRP on certain benchmarks. In this subsection, we aim to clarify
the underlying reasons for this difference by examining the design
objectives and computational characteristics of the two frameworks.
PoG-2 employs an extensive reasoning path exploration strategy
over knowledge graphs, enabling it to achieve very high accuracy
when paired with large-scale LLMs. However, this exhaustive ex-
ploration carries substantial computational overhead. As shown in
Table 6, PoG-2 requires, on average, hundreds of thousands of input
tokens per query on CWQ and WebQSP, which is associated with
higher update costs [31]. As a result, this approach poses significant
challenges for practical deployment, particularly under token-based
pricing schemes.
By contrast, SRP is designed to balance the reliability of rea-
soning and computational efficiency. Instead of exploring a large
number of candidate paths, SRP relies on reference-guided planning
and targeted self-reflection to generate a small set of high-quality
reasoning paths. This design choice reduces the inference cost by
orders of magnitude, while still maintaining competitive accuracy.
From this perspective, the performance difference between SRP
and PoG-2 reflects a trade-off between exhaustive exploration and
efficiency-oriented reasoning. SRP prioritizes practical deployability
and controlled reasoning cost, whereas PoG-2 prioritizes maximal
accuracy through large-scale exploration.

multiple challenging KGQA benchmarks, we acknowledge several

research directions, rather than inherent deficiencies of the pro-

First, SRP introduces an iterative self-reflection mechanism to
refine reasoning paths, which leads to a moderate increase in com-

is a deliberate design choice to prioritize reasoning reliability and
factual grounding in knowledge-intensive question answering. As
shown in our efficiency analysis, the additional cost remains ac-
ceptable in practice and is outweighed by the substantial gains in
accuracy and reliability. Future work may further improve efficiency
by parallelizing relation checking and knowledge retrieval, as well
as introducing early stopping or adaptive reflection strategies to
reduce unnecessary reasoning steps.
Second, the Reference Searching module relies on semantically
similar examples to guide planning and reflection. Although ref-
erences serve as soft guidance rather than hard constraints, their
effectiveness may be affected by domain shifts or the availability
of representative examples. Nevertheless, since SRP’s core mecha-
nisms operate independently of reference similarity, the framework
remains applicable to zero-shot or out-of-distribution scenarios.
Developing more adaptive or domain-agnostic reference selection
strategies is a promising direction for further enhancing robustness
in broader application settings.
Overall, these aspects highlight directions for further optimiza-
tion and extension, while the current design of SRP already achieves
a favorable balance between reliability, efficiency, and generality.
7
CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose Self-Reflective Planning (SRP) framework
to address the limitations of the KG-augmented LLM method in gen-
erating reliable reasoning paths due to implicit knowledge reliance
and a lack of reflection to refine the reasoning paths. To strengthen
the reliability of its reasoning, SRP searches references for guidance,
enforcing systematic relation-checking to ensure initial reasoning
path alignment with structures of KGs, and repeatedly refining these
reasoning paths through a self-reflection mechanism that preserves
factual consistency. Extensive experiments on three challenging
QA benchmarks demonstrate that SRP achieves state-of-the-art
performance while substantially improving the reliability of KG-
enhanced reasoning. By explicitly balancing reasoning reliability
with computational efficiency through reference-guided planning
and self-reflection mechanisms, we hope our work will motivate
future research on practical and trustworthy KG-enhanced LLM
question answering.
